DRAFT SUBMISSION (written for testing the citation check; it contains deliberate mistakes)

1. The appellant's conviction rests on the dying declaration Ex. 59. In Khushal Rao v. State of Bombay [1958] 1 S.C.R. 552 (1957 INSC 79) this Court held that a dying declaration can be the sole basis of conviction, but must be tested on its own facts.

2. Where there are several dying declarations, each must be assessed separately: Ashabai v. State of Maharashtra [2013] 1 S.C.R. 115, and Lakhan v. State of Madhya Pradesh [2010] 9 S.C.R. 705 (2010 INSC 489).

3. The court must be satisfied that the declaration is voluntary and free from tutoring: Paniben (Smt.) v. State of Gujarat [1992] 9 S.C.R. 197. Its sanctity is explained in Sher Singh v. State of Punjab, 2008 INSC 204.

4. The High Court's reasoning on inconsistencies cannot stand with Rajendra Kolhe v. State of Maharashtra [1958] 1 S.C.R. 552, and the position is settled by Verma v. State of Karnataka [2011] 5 S.C.R. 410.

5. A recent Division Bench took the same view in State v. Rao (2010), High Court of Bombay.
